% ---------- Abstract ----------
\chapter*{ABSTRACT}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{ABSTRACT}

Sidewalk-scale autonomy demands perception and planning systems that run reliably on compact, low-power hardware in cluttered, map-sparse environments. This thesis presents a monocular vision pipeline for sidewalk-following micro-mobility that progresses through three design iterations---from a skeleton-graph baseline through distance-transform corridor planning to a lightweight image-space architecture---and provides a systematic comparison of five path-planning methods across both bird's-eye view (BEV) and image-space domains.

A compact SegFormer-B0 student model (3.7M parameters), trained with a semi-supervised teacher--student framework using OneFormer Swin-L pseudo-labels, achieves a hand-annotated intersection-over-union (IoU) of 0.946 at \SI{11.7}{ms} per frame, improving over the baseline checkpoint (IoU 0.758, \SI{18.9}{ms}). In a controlled planner comparison on 32 hand-labeled frames from University of Oklahoma campus sidewalks, image-space midpoint planning achieves the lowest lateral center error (\SI{14.3}{\px}) at \SI{2.2}{ms}---a $421\times$ speedup over BEV distance-transform planning (\SI{926.8}{ms}, \SI{65.0}{\px} center error)---while maintaining comparable mask--path alignment (98.5\% vs.\ 98.6\%). A full-video replay across six campus sequences (\numprint{22679} frames) confirms that the improved segmentation reduces temporal instability from 1.46\% to 0.33\% and increases template-planner success rate from 73.7\% to 79.3\%.

A template arc planner further reduces mean heading error by 40.6\% (from $1.190^\circ$ to $0.707^\circ$) and halves path-source switches compared to the skeleton-graph baseline by selecting from a fixed arc bank with family-reuse hysteresis. For GPS-conditioned junction maneuvers, a waypoint-turn planner detects asymmetric road openings in the BEV mask and generates constant-curvature turn arcs gated by dual containment thresholds. A turn containment safety guard verifies the final control path against the BEV mask before execution, achieving 0\% containment failure rate in overnight validation. An 1800-frame accepted run achieves 100\% template success across all frames at a mean segmentation IoU of 0.954 and 9.97~FPS.

This thesis further demonstrates that BEV-only planning is fundamentally fragile in monocular settings: in one profiled run, 99.3\% of frames produced no valid BEV path due to insufficient mask coverage from the monocular viewpoint. The final recommended architecture---image-space midpoint primary, image-space distance-transform fallback, BEV reserved for visualization and turn planning---runs the full perception-to-path stack in under \SI{17}{ms} per frame on CPU (59~FPS), exceeding the 10~Hz real-time requirement for pedestrian-speed platforms.

The system further integrates YOLOv8-nano obstacle detection, GPS waypoint navigation, and a serial command protocol for closed-loop scooter control, validated through live campus demonstrations.

\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:introduction}
% =====================================================================

\section{Motivation}

Autonomous micro-mobility is beginning to move from controlled demonstrations to everyday sidewalks. Delivery carts, assistive scooters, and other pedestrian-speed robots must operate among pedestrians, signs, bicycles, vegetation, and irregular curb geometry---all under tight payload, power, and cost constraints. Sidewalks further complicate perception: widths vary from under one meter to over three meters, surfaces mix bricks and concrete, markings are inconsistent, and lighting changes rapidly under trees and buildings. High-definition pedestrian maps are scarce and GNSS is unreliable near canopies and urban canyons. In this setting, a vision-first navigation pipeline that is robust, interpretable, and efficient enough for single-board computers is essential.

The economic and social potential of autonomous sidewalk vehicles is substantial. Last-mile delivery consumes a disproportionate share of logistics costs, and autonomous delivery robots could reduce this burden while decreasing carbon emissions from delivery vans~\cite{starship2022}. Assistive mobility scooters could extend independence for individuals with mobility limitations. Campus and airport shuttle vehicles could improve pedestrian flow in dense environments. All of these applications share a common technical requirement: the ability to perceive the traversable path ahead and generate safe navigation commands in real time, using sensors and compute that fit within the form factor and power budget of a small electric vehicle.

Unlike structured road environments where lane markings and traffic signs provide rich cues, sidewalk environments are inherently unstructured. The traversable region must be inferred from appearance and geometry rather than from painted boundaries. Furthermore, the computational budget available to a battery-powered scooter or delivery robot is a fraction of what a full-size autonomous vehicle can afford. These constraints motivate a deliberately simple, geometry-aware approach that favors transparency and efficiency over raw model capacity.


\section{Problem Statement}

This thesis addresses the problem of autonomous sidewalk-following navigation for micro-mobility platforms using only a single forward-facing monocular camera. Specifically, we seek a system that:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Segments traversable sidewalk regions from RGB video in real time on CPU-only hardware.
  \item Extracts a centered, smooth navigation path from the segmentation mask.
  \item Operates in either bird's-eye view (BEV) or image-space domains, with empirical evidence for which is superior.
  \item Achieves real-time performance ($\geq$10~Hz) on embedded single-board computers.
  \item Extends to closed-loop control with obstacle detection and GPS waypoint navigation.
\end{enumerate}


\section{Approach Overview}

We adopt a modular pipeline that maps camera frames to controller-ready waypoints through interpretable stages: (i)~semantic segmentation via a lightweight SegFormer-B0 model trained with a teacher--student framework, (ii)~optional Bird's-Eye View (BEV) projection via a fixed calibrated homography, (iii)~mask refinement and component filtering, (iv)~path extraction via one of five planning methods spanning BEV and image-space domains, and (v)~temporal smoothing and control output.

The system evolved through three design iterations, each backward-compatible with the previous:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Iteration~1:} BEV skeleton-graph planner with SegFormer-B2 teacher.
  \item \textbf{Iteration~2:} BEV distance-transform corridor planner with enhanced mask refinement.
  \item \textbf{Iteration~3:} Image-space midpoint and DT planners with OneFormer Swin-L teacher, eliminating BEV dependency for path extraction.
  \item \textbf{Iteration~4:} Template arc planner with GPS-conditioned waypoint-turn planner and turn containment safety guard, adding junction maneuver capability with verified path safety.
\end{itemize}

This direction complements several active research threads. End-to-end steering policies can perform well but are difficult to interpret and often assume GPU-class hardware~\cite{e2e_nav_policies, viteri2024}. Dense monocular BEV reconstruction pushes accuracy but remains challenging to deploy on low-power platforms~\cite{zhao2024bev}. Classic semantic-mask corridor following is efficient in structured environments but does not explicitly expose intersection topology~\cite{orchard_seg_nav, row_nav_survey}. In contrast, our approach is monocular, transparent, and light enough for CPU-only inference, while providing an empirical comparison of planning-domain trade-offs that is absent from prior sidewalk navigation work.

In this thesis, we focus on validating the perception and path-generation pipeline offline using video recorded from a forward-facing camera mounted on an electric scooter (Figure~\ref{fig:scooter_hw}). We apply the full pipeline frame by frame and evaluate the resulting paths without closing the loop on the vehicle. This isolates perception and planning, enabling detailed analysis of segmentation quality, planner accuracy, runtime efficiency, and the BEV-versus-image-space trade-off.


\section{Contributions}

This thesis makes the following contributions:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item A modular monocular sidewalk navigation pipeline that is transparent, lightweight, and designed for embedded hardware, progressing through three design iterations with backward-compatible improvements.

  \item A semi-supervised segmentation training recipe using a high-capacity OneFormer Swin-L teacher~\cite{oneformer} to generate pseudo-labels for a compact SegFormer-B0 student~\cite{segformer}, achieving hand-annotated IoU of 0.946 at \SI{11.7}{ms} on CPU.

  \item A systematic comparison of five path-planning methods across BEV and image-space domains, demonstrating that image-space midpoint planning achieves a $421\times$ speedup over BEV distance-transform planning with lower lateral error.

  \item Empirical evidence that BEV-only planning is fragile in monocular sidewalk settings, with 99.3\% of frames failing to produce a valid BEV path in one profiled sequence, motivating image-space planning as the primary domain.

  \item A comprehensive offline evaluation protocol covering segmentation quality, planner accuracy, temporal stability, and runtime analysis across six campus video sequences totaling over \numprint{22000} frames.

  \item Integration of YOLOv8-nano obstacle detection~\cite{yolov8}, GPS waypoint navigation, and a serial command protocol for closed-loop scooter control.

  \item A GPS-conditioned waypoint-turn planner with a turn containment safety guard that verifies committed control paths against the BEV mask before execution, validated through overnight replay evaluation with 0\% containment failure rate and 12.9~FPS throughput.
\end{enumerate}

\chapter{Background and Related Work}
\label{ch:background}
% =====================================================================

This chapter reviews prior work organized into eight themes, emphasizing what each direction offers in practice---what runs fast, what transfers, and what stays explainable.


\section{Autonomous Navigation for Micro-Mobility Platforms}

The deployment of autonomous micro-mobility vehicles on sidewalks and shared pedestrian paths represents a growing research frontier distinct from road-driving autonomy. Starship Technologies has deployed thousands of delivery robots that navigate sidewalks using a combination of cameras, ultrasonic sensors, and pre-mapped routes~\cite{starship2022}. Academic efforts include RGB-D dual-ConvNet steering for sidewalk-following scooters~\cite{viteri2024} and monocular deep-learning navigation systems for ground robots~\cite{machkour2023}. Target-driven visual navigation systems in indoor environments combine monocular depth and segmentation to follow objects without explicit mapping~\cite{zhu2017target}.

A key distinction from road-driving autonomy is the sensor budget. Road vehicles typically employ LiDAR arrays costing thousands of dollars and GPU clusters consuming hundreds of watts. Sidewalk robots must achieve comparable reliability with cameras costing tens of dollars and single-board computers consuming under 15~watts. This constraint motivates modular, geometry-based approaches that can be inspected and tuned without retraining deep models.


\section{Semantic Segmentation for Drivable Surfaces}
\label{sec:lit_segmentation}

Semantic segmentation has evolved rapidly from fully convolutional networks~\cite{fcn2015} through encoder-decoder architectures like DeepLabV3+~\cite{deeplabv3plus} to transformer-based models. SegFormer~\cite{segformer} introduced a hierarchical Vision Transformer encoder with a lightweight MLP decoder, achieving strong performance across urban benchmarks while scaling efficiently from B0 (3.7M parameters) to B5 (84.7M parameters). For sidewalk segmentation specifically, ensemble approaches using multiple backbones can improve mIoU but increase computational cost~\cite{shihab2024}. Lightweight alternatives optimized for real-time deployment include MobileNetV3~\cite{edge_optimized_seg}, TwinLiteNet~\cite{twinlitenet2024}, BiSeNetV2~\cite{bisenetv2}, and DDRNet~\cite{ddrnet}.

Sidewalk-specific segmentation datasets remain limited compared to road-driving datasets. CityScapes~\cite{cityscapes} includes sidewalk labels but focuses on European urban driving scenes. The Robot Unstructured Ground Dataset (RUGD)~\cite{rugd} provides off-road terrain segmentation relevant to unstructured path following. Mapillary Vistas~\cite{mapillary} offers diverse street-level imagery with sidewalk annotations. The scarcity of dedicated sidewalk segmentation benchmarks motivates domain-specific training approaches such as the teacher--student framework employed in this thesis.


\section{Bird's-Eye View Projection and Monocular BEV}
\label{sec:lit_bev}

Bird's-eye view (BEV) representations provide a spatially consistent top-down frame that simplifies metric reasoning for planning. The classical approach uses a planar homography (Inverse Perspective Mapping, IPM) to warp the camera image onto the ground plane~\cite{hartley_zisserman}. This assumes a flat surface and known camera extrinsics---assumptions that hold approximately for sidewalks but break on ramps, curbs, and uneven terrain.

Recent work has moved toward learned BEV representations. Lift, Splat, Shoot~\cite{lift_splat_shoot} encodes images from arbitrary camera rigs by implicitly unprojecting to 3D. BEVFormer~\cite{bevformer} uses spatiotemporal transformers to construct BEV features from multi-camera input. Focus on BEV~\cite{zhao2024bev} introduces self-calibrated cycle view transformation for monocular BEV segmentation. However, these learned approaches require substantial GPU compute and are designed for multi-camera setups on full-size vehicles, making them impractical for single-camera embedded platforms.

A critical limitation of monocular BEV that has received insufficient attention is \emph{coverage fragility}: a single forward-facing camera maps only a narrow strip of the ground plane into the BEV grid, leaving most of the BEV frame empty. When the segmented mask is sparse or noisy, the projected BEV occupancy can drop to near zero, causing downstream planners to fail entirely. This thesis provides the first quantitative characterization of this failure mode for sidewalk navigation (Section~\ref{sec:bev_fragility_results}).


\section{Path Planning for Mobile Robots}
\label{sec:lit_planning}

Local path planning for mobile robots encompasses a broad spectrum of methods. Grid-based planners such as A* and Dijkstra operate on discretized cost maps and guarantee optimal paths within the grid resolution~\cite{lavalle2006}. Sampling-based methods (RRT, PRM) excel in high-dimensional configuration spaces but are less relevant for 2D mask corridors. Potential field methods use attractive and repulsive forces to guide the robot~\cite{khatib1986}. End-to-end steering policies trained via imitation learning or reinforcement learning can map directly from images to control~\cite{e2e_nav_policies, bojarski2016}.

For sidewalk navigation specifically, most prior work operates in BEV or world coordinates. Agricultural row-following uses histogram-of-column methods that are fast and reliable in structured corridors~\cite{orchard_seg_nav, row_nav_survey}. Our work introduces the alternative of planning directly in image space---bypassing BEV entirely---and provides the first systematic comparison of BEV-domain and image-space planning methods for monocular sidewalk navigation.


\section{Distance Transform Methods in Navigation}

The Euclidean distance transform (EDT) computes, for each pixel in a binary mask, the distance to the nearest obstacle (non-traversable) pixel. This produces a natural clearance map where the ridge (local maxima) traces the centerline of the corridor---the path of maximum clearance. In robotics, EDT-based approaches are widely used for safe corridor extraction~\cite{felzenszwalb_dt}. The Euclidean Signed Distance Field (ESDF) extends this concept to 3D for aerial and manipulator planning.

In 2D mask planning, the EDT ridge can be traced via dynamic programming or Dijkstra search on a cost field $c(x,y) = 1/(d(x,y) + \varepsilon)^\alpha$, where $d$ is the EDT value. This produces clearance-optimal paths without the noise sensitivity of morphological skeletonization. We employ this approach in both our BEV DT planner and image-space DT planner, providing a controlled comparison of the same algorithm applied in different coordinate domains.


\section{Skeletonization and Topological Path Extraction}

Morphological skeletonization reduces a binary region to its 1-pixel-wide medial axis, preserving topological structure (branches, junctions). The Zhang--Suen algorithm~\cite{zhang_suen} and its parallel variant by Guo and Hall~\cite{guo_hall} are the most widely used thinning methods. From the skeleton, a graph can be constructed with pixels as nodes and 8-neighbor connections as edges, enabling efficient path enumeration via graph search.

Learned skeletonization~\cite{flores2025skeleton} can substantially reduce planning cost in simulation. However, classical thinning remains attractive in embedded contexts because it is stable, deterministic, and requires no learned parameters. A known limitation is noise sensitivity: small protrusions in the input mask generate spurious branches that require pruning. This thesis uses Guo--Hall skeletonization as one of the five compared planning methods and documents its computational cost relative to alternatives.


\section{Semi-Supervised and Teacher--Student Learning}
\label{sec:lit_teacher_student}

Knowledge distillation~\cite{hinton2015distilling} transfers knowledge from a large ``teacher'' model to a compact ``student'' model. For semantic segmentation, this is particularly valuable because annotation is expensive: labeling pixel-level masks takes 10--30$\times$ longer than classification labels. Semi-supervised approaches use the teacher to generate pseudo-labels on unlabeled data, which are then used to train the student~\cite{pseudolabel_lee2013}.

OneFormer~\cite{oneformer} is a universal image segmentation model that unifies semantic, instance, and panoptic segmentation in a single architecture. Pre-trained on large-scale datasets (ADE20K, COCO, CityScapes), OneFormer with a Swin-L backbone provides high-quality dense predictions that serve as effective pseudo-labels for domain-specific student training. This thesis uses OneFormer Swin-L as the teacher and SegFormer-B0 as the student, a combination that has not been previously applied to sidewalk segmentation.


\section{Embedded and Real-Time Perception}
\label{sec:lit_embedded}

Deploying neural networks on embedded hardware requires careful attention to model size, inference latency, and memory footprint. Single-board computers such as the Raspberry Pi 4 (4-core ARM Cortex-A72, 2--8~GB RAM) and Rock 5B (8-core ARM, 8--16~GB RAM) provide limited computational resources compared to desktop GPUs. ONNX Runtime~\cite{onnxruntime} enables cross-platform model deployment with optimized CPU and GPU backends. TensorRT provides NVIDIA-specific acceleration on Jetson platforms.

For pedestrian-speed platforms, the latency requirement is relatively relaxed. Human visual reaction time is approximately 200~ms~\cite{mental_chronometry_react}, and typical camera-based robot control frequencies range from 2--10~Hz~\cite{pi0_lowfreq}. A 10~Hz (100~ms) pipeline is generally considered sufficient for platforms moving at 1--2~m/s. This thesis demonstrates that the recommended image-space architecture achieves 59~FPS on CPU---far exceeding this requirement and providing substantial headroom for additional processing.


\section{Summary and Research Gap}

Prior work in sidewalk navigation has primarily focused on either end-to-end approaches requiring depth sensors or GPU compute, or BEV-based geometric methods that assume reliable BEV projection. The following gaps motivate this thesis:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item No prior systematic comparison of BEV-domain and image-space path planning for monocular sidewalk navigation.
  \item Limited empirical characterization of when monocular BEV projection helps versus when it introduces failure.
  \item The teacher--student combination of OneFormer Swin-L and SegFormer-B0 has not been applied to sidewalk-specific binary segmentation.
  \item Few complete perception-to-planning pipelines have been benchmarked end-to-end on CPU-only embedded hardware for sidewalk applications.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Hardware Platform}
\label{sec:hardware}

The experimental platform is an electric scooter equipped with a single forward-facing monocular RGB camera rigidly mounted to the handlebar stem (Figure~\ref{fig:scooter_hw}). The camera captures 1920$\times$1080 video at 30~FPS. No depth sensor, stereo camera, LiDAR, or IMU is used. The target deployment hardware is a Raspberry Pi~4 or Rock~5B class single-board computer; all development and evaluation were conducted on a workstation with an Intel Core~i7 CPU and NVIDIA GPU, with runtime measurements reported for CPU-only inference to reflect embedded deployment conditions.


% -----------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Segmentation Module and Supervision Strategy}
\label{sec:segmentation}

The first stage performs pixel-wise segmentation of sidewalk regions from monocular RGB input. This module is critical for all downstream steps and must balance segmentation quality with real-time efficiency on embedded systems.

We adopt \textbf{SegFormer}~\cite{segformer} as our backbone architecture due to its strong performance on urban benchmarks and adaptability across model scales. Unlike conventional encoder--decoder networks, SegFormer combines a hierarchical Transformer-based encoder with a multi-scale MLP decoder, achieving competitive accuracy without positional encodings or complex upsampling. For deployment, we use \textbf{SegFormer-B0} (3.7M parameters) for its low latency and memory footprint.


\subsection{Teacher--Student Supervision}
\label{sec:teacher_student}

Small models trained on limited data often produce noisy masks. To mitigate this, we employ a semi-supervised teacher--student framework. We explored two teacher architectures across our design iterations:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Iteration~1:} A SegFormer-B2 teacher (24M parameters) fine-tuned on 300 hand-labeled frames, generating pseudo-labels on 2,300 unlabeled frames.
  \item \textbf{Iteration~2:} A high-capacity \textbf{OneFormer Swin-L} teacher~\cite{oneformer} pre-trained on ADE20K, generating dense binary pseudo-labels from up to 1,419 unlabeled frames across 10 campus video sequences.
\end{itemize}

For each unlabeled image $x \in D_U$, the teacher predicts pixel-wise logits $z_T = f_T(x)$, converted to probabilities via softmax: $p_T = \sigma(z_T)$. A confidence threshold $\tau = 0.60$ produces binary masks:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{y}(u) =
\begin{cases}
1,& \text{if } p_T(u) \ge \tau \\
0,& \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\quad \text{with } u \in \Omega
\end{equation}
where $\Omega$ denotes the set of pixel locations. Pseudo-labels are further cleaned with connected-component filtering and morphological smoothing to remove spurious isolated predictions.


\subsection{Hybrid Training Dataset}

The training set $\mathcal{D} = D_L \cup D_P$ merges pseudo-labeled frames $D_P$ with hand-labeled frames $D_L$. In frames where both are available, hand labels take precedence:
\begin{equation}
y_i =
\begin{cases}
y_i^{\text{hand}}, & \text{if hand-labeled} \\
\tilde{y}_i^{\text{pseudo}}, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Our best-performing student was trained on 1,419 image--mask pairs (699 from earlier sequences plus 720 newly extracted frames from four additional campus videos).


\subsection{Loss Function}

The student model $f_S$ minimizes a composite loss:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \lambda_{\text{CE}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}} + \lambda_{\text{Dice}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{Dice}} + \lambda_{\text{B}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{boundary}}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}$ is binary cross-entropy, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Dice}}$ improves mask completeness in sparse regions, and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{boundary}}$ sharpens sidewalk edges by upweighting boundary pixels.

\section{Bird's-Eye View Projection}
\label{sec:bev}

To transform the segmented mask into a spatially consistent top-down representation, we apply a planar homography. Let $\mathbf{p} = [u, v, 1]^\top$ be a homogeneous image pixel and $\mathbf{p}' = [x, y, 1]^\top$ its BEV coordinate. The mapping is:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{p}' \sim H \mathbf{p}
\end{equation}
where $H$ is a $3\times3$ homography matrix estimated via four point correspondences between the camera image and a flat ground reference. The resulting BEV mask is aligned with the robot's reference frame, with the ego position at the bottom-center and forward motion along the vertical axis.

The BEV grid covers a $10\text{m} \times 10\text{m}$ area at $600 \times 600$ pixels, yielding a scale of 60~px/m. Calibration is performed using an interactive tool that allows the operator to select four ground-plane correspondences for any camera mounting angle. This transformation provides metric-scale reasoning but assumes a flat plane and fixed camera pose---assumptions we test empirically in Section~\ref{sec:bev_fragility_results}.


% -----------------------------------------------------------------
\section{BEV Mask Refinement}
\label{sec:mask_refinement}

The raw BEV mask often contains noise: thin spurs, disconnected blobs, and boundary artifacts from segmentation errors amplified by the perspective warp. We apply a multi-stage cleanup pipeline:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Morphological closing and opening} with configurable kernel sizes suppress small gaps and spurs.
  \item \textbf{Connected-component filtering} retains only the largest connected region touching the ego anchor row.
  \item \textbf{Enhanced refinement} (optional): flood-fill hole filling from corners to identify enclosed holes that standard morphological operations cannot reach; Gaussian boundary smoothing to reduce jagged edges; and distance-transform-based component selection that preferentially retains the component with the highest ego clearance rather than simply the largest area.
\end{enumerate}

These stages are individually toggleable via configuration flags, allowing controlled ablation.

\section{Path Planning Methods}
\label{sec:planners}

We compare five path planning methods spanning two geometric domains: three operating in BEV space and two in image space. All methods receive a binary sidewalk mask and produce a centerline trajectory.


\subsection{BEV Skeleton-Graph Planner}
\label{sec:skeleton_planner}

The Guo--Hall parallel thinning algorithm~\cite{guo_hall} reduces the BEV mask to a 1-pixel-wide skeleton preserving topological structure. From this skeleton, we construct an undirected graph $\mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E})$ where each skeleton pixel is a node and 8-neighbor connections form edges. Two-stage pruning (length-based and component filtering) suppresses spurious branches. Dijkstra's algorithm enumerates candidate paths from the ego node $v_0$, each scored by:
\begin{equation}
C(p_j) = \alpha \cdot \text{Curvature}(p_j) + \beta \cdot \text{LateralShift}(p_j)
\end{equation}
This method explicitly exposes junction topology but is computationally expensive due to per-pixel graph construction.


\subsection{BEV Distance-Transform Ridge Planner}
\label{sec:dt_planner}

Rather than skeletonize, this method computes the Euclidean distance transform (EDT) of the BEV mask and traces the ridge of maximum clearance. The cost field
\begin{equation}
c(x,y) = \frac{1}{(d(x,y) + \varepsilon)^\alpha}
\end{equation}
where $d$ is the EDT value, $\varepsilon = 0.5$, and $\alpha = 1.5$, is minimized via Dijkstra's algorithm to find the globally maximum-clearance path. The resulting centerline is smoothed with a Savitzky--Golay filter (window size 9, polynomial order 2). This approach avoids the noise sensitivity of skeletonization but inherits the full cost of BEV computation plus the EDT and graph search.


\subsection{BEV Template Arc Planner}
\label{sec:template_planner}

A bank of predefined arc templates (straight, gentle-left, gentle-right, sharp-left, sharp-right) is scored against a corridor extracted from the DT ridge. Each template is evaluated by its containment within the segmented corridor and its smoothness. The highest-scoring template that passes a confidence gate becomes the planned path. This method trades generality for speed and interpretability: the arc bank is fixed and small, making runtime predictable.


\subsection{Image-Space Midpoint Planner}
\label{sec:img_midpoint}

This method operates directly on the image-plane segmentation mask $\widehat{M}_\text{img}$, bypassing BEV entirely. For each row $y$ of the mask, the left and right sidewalk boundaries are identified and the midpoint $x_\text{mid}(y) = (x_L(y) + x_R(y))/2$ is computed. Rows with fewer than a minimum number of road pixels are skipped. A connected-component filter ensures only the ego-connected mask region is used. The resulting per-row midpoints are smoothed with a Savitzky--Golay filter to produce a continuous centerline. This method is the fastest (\SI{2.2}{ms}) and produces the lowest lateral center error in our experiments.


\subsection{Image-Space Distance-Transform Planner}
\label{sec:img_dt}

The EDT is computed on the image-plane mask, and the maximum-distance ridge is traced using dynamic programming with a lateral drift constraint. A per-row forward pass from the ego (bottom-center) selects the column with minimum cost (inverse EDT) subject to a maximum lateral step per row, producing a path that tracks the widest part of the sidewalk corridor. This method is more robust than midpoint extraction on masks with irregular boundaries or partial gaps but slower (\SI{108.1}{ms}).


\subsection{GPS-Conditioned Waypoint-Turn Planner}
\label{sec:waypoint_turn_method}

When a GPS waypoint triggers a junction maneuver (left or right intent), the system activates a dedicated waypoint-turn planner that uses the BEV mask to detect and execute the commanded turn. The planner operates in five stages:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Decision band scan.} The raw BEV mask is scanned in a forward decision band (\SIrange{2}{7}{m} ahead) to detect asymmetric road openings on the commanded side. Rows where the commanded-side pixel count exceeds the opposite side by a minimum asymmetry margin are classified as ``supported.''
  \item \textbf{Support clustering.} Supported rows are grouped into contiguous clusters (forward gap $\leq$ \SI{0.5}{m}), and the largest cluster's midpoint becomes the candidate turn apex.
  \item \textbf{Target gating.} The support score (fraction of decision-band rows with commanded-side support) must exceed an acquisition threshold of 0.40 for initial lock and 0.25 for sustain. A lock hysteresis of 3 frames to acquire and 5 frames to release prevents flicker.
  \item \textbf{Circular arc path.} A constant-curvature circular arc is fitted from the ego position through the apex, parameterized as $x = R\sin\phi$, $y = \pm R(1-\cos\phi)$ where $R$ is solved from the endpoint geometry. The arc does not attempt to rejoin the corridor centerline within the planning horizon; rejoin occurs naturally as new frames arrive.
  \item \textbf{Containment gating.} The generated path must satisfy two containment thresholds: $\geq$60\% of all path points inside the corridor, and $\geq$70\% of points within the near-field (\SI{1.2}{m}). Paths failing either gate are rejected in favor of hold behavior with speed reduction.
\end{enumerate}

The overall confidence is a weighted combination of support score, containment ratio, near-field containment, and corridor confidence, used to modulate speed during the maneuver.


\subsection{Turn Containment Safety Guard}
\label{sec:turn_containment_method}

As a final safety layer, the runtime evaluates every committed control path against the actual BEV drivable mask before execution. For each sampled point on the control path, the guard measures:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Path-outside ratio}: fraction of path samples falling outside the BEV mask.
  \item \textbf{Path-outside count}: absolute number of samples outside the mask.
  \item \textbf{Minimum boundary clearance}: signed distance-transform value at each sample (negative when outside the mask boundary).
\end{itemize}

The first \texttt{TURN\_PATH\_IGNORE\_FIRST\_SAMPLES\,=\,4} samples near the ego are excluded from evaluation because the BEV bottom-edge zone exhibits mask noise when ego anchoring is disabled. When a turn path fails containment, the system falls back to hold behavior with full speed reduction, ensuring that unsafe paths never reach the motor controller.

\section{Temporal Smoothing}
\label{sec:temporal_smoothing}

Frame-by-frame path extraction produces jittery trajectories due to segmentation noise. We apply two temporal filters:

\paragraph{Path temporal smoothing.}
An exponential moving average (EMA) is applied to the cubic polynomial coefficients $[a_0, a_1, a_2, a_3]$ of the fitted path. The smoothing factor $\alpha$ is adaptive: higher confidence paths receive less smoothing ($\alpha \to 0.85$), while low-confidence paths receive more ($\alpha \to 0.35$). A topology-change detector resets the filter when the maximum per-coefficient jump exceeds a threshold, preventing averaging across distinct path segments.

\paragraph{Heading temporal smoothing.}
A circular EMA filter on the heading angle handles the $\pm 180^\circ$ wraparound discontinuity. The filter resets when the heading delta exceeds $45^\circ$, allowing rapid response to sharp turns while suppressing noise on straight segments.

\paragraph{Mask temporal smoothing.}
A camera-space EMA with a conservative-blend policy reduces the effective $\alpha$ when the consecutive-frame IoU falls below a consistency threshold, since a large IoU drop signals a probable camera shake or sudden scene change:
\begin{equation}
\hat{M}_t^{\text{cam}} = \alpha_{\text{eff}} \cdot M_t + (1 - \alpha_{\text{eff}}) \cdot \hat{M}_{t-1}^{\text{cam}}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha = 0.65$ and consistency threshold $c_\text{thresh} = 0.20$.

\section{Lightweight Object Detection}
\label{sec:object_detection}

Dynamic obstacles---pedestrians, cyclists, dogs, and vehicles---are a primary safety concern. We adopt YOLOv8-nano~\cite{yolov8} (3.2M parameters, $\sim$\SI{6}{MB}) from the Ultralytics framework, pretrained on COCO~\cite{coco} (80 classes). At inference, predictions are filtered to retain eight safety-relevant classes: person, bicycle, car, motorcycle, bus, truck, cat, and dog. A confidence threshold of $\tau_\text{det} = 0.35$ balances recall and false-positive suppression.

\subsection{Monocular Distance Estimation}

Without stereo or depth sensors, we estimate object distance from the vertical position of the bounding-box bottom edge using a pinhole-camera approximation:
\begin{equation}
d \approx \frac{h_c}{\tan\!\bigl(\phi_v \cdot (y_{\text{foot}}/H - 0.5)\bigr)}
\end{equation}
where $h_c = \SI{0.8}{m}$ is the camera mounting height, $\phi_v = \SI{55}{\degree}$ is the vertical field of view, $y_\text{foot}$ is the pixel row of the bounding-box bottom, and $H$ is the frame height. The estimate is clamped to $[0.5, 20]$~m.

\subsection{Speed Modulation}

The nearest obstacle distance $d_\text{min}$ modulates the speed setpoint:
\begin{equation}
v_{\text{obs}} =
\begin{cases}
0, & d_{\min} < d_{\text{stop}} = \SI{1.0}{m} \\
v_{\text{slow}} = \SI{0.3}{m/s}, & d_{\text{stop}} \le d_{\min} < d_{\text{close}} = \SI{3.0}{m} \\
v_{\text{nominal}}, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}


\section{GPS Waypoint Navigation}
\label{sec:gps_navigation}

The vision-based pipeline provides \emph{local} path-following. GPS provides the \emph{global} context for branch selection at junctions. A low-cost GNSS module streams standard NMEA-0183 sentences via USB-serial. A background thread parses \texttt{\$GPGGA} and \texttt{\$GPRMC} sentences. The operator supplies a route as a CSV file of GPS waypoints.

The final heading command blends local vision heading $\theta_\text{vis}$ and global GPS correction $\theta_\text{gps}$:
\begin{equation}
\theta = w_v \cdot \theta_{\text{vis}} + w_g \cdot \theta_{\text{gps}}, \qquad w_v = 0.7,\ w_g = 0.3
\end{equation}
When no GPS fix is available, $w_g$ falls to zero and the system reverts to pure vision control.


\section{Steering and Speed Computation}
\label{sec:steer_speed}

The heading angle $\theta_\text{vis}$ is computed from the planned path as the angle between the forward direction and the vector from the ego to a point approximately 40\% along the path. This is converted to both a discrete command and a continuous steering angle:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item $|\theta| < 12^\circ$: \textbf{STRAIGHT} ($v = \SI{1.5}{m/s}$)
  \item $12^\circ \le |\theta| < 40^\circ$: \textbf{LEFT/RIGHT} ($v = 1.5 - (1.5 - 0.8) \cdot \frac{|\theta|-12}{28}$ m/s)
  \item $|\theta| \ge 40^\circ$: \textbf{SHARP LEFT/RIGHT} ($v = \SI{0.4}{m/s}$)
\end{itemize}

Both heading and speed are temporally smoothed. Both the heading angle and speed command are smoothed with EMA or rolling-window filters to remove high-frequency jitter.


\section{Serial Command Protocol}
\label{sec:serial_protocol}

The fused steering angle and smoothed speed are transmitted to the scooter's motor controller as a simple ASCII line protocol:
\begin{verbatim}
    CMD,<steer_deg>,<speed_mps>\n
\end{verbatim}
A watchdog on the scooter side triggers a safety stop if no valid command is received within \SI{500}{ms}.


\section{Safety Mechanisms}
\label{sec:safety}

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{No-path stop:} Full stop if no valid path for $N$ consecutive frames.
  \item \textbf{Obstacle stop/slow:} Immediate stop within \SI{1.0}{m}; slowdown within \SI{3.0}{m}.
  \item \textbf{GPS loss fallback:} Graceful degradation to vision-only control.
  \item \textbf{Manual override:} Emergency stop via hardware button or keyboard.
  \item \textbf{Segmentation instability gate:} Speed cap (\SI{0.2}{m/s}) when segmentation IoU drops below threshold for multiple consecutive frames.
\end{itemize}

\section{Evaluation Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}

We evaluate the pipeline offline and define five quantitative metrics:

\paragraph{Segmentation IoU.}
Intersection-over-union between the predicted binary mask and hand-annotated ground truth, computed per-frame and averaged.

\paragraph{Lateral path-centering error.}
For each sample point along the planned path, we measure the lateral deviation from the geometric centerline of the ground-truth sidewalk mask. Reported in pixels.

\paragraph{Mask--path alignment (inside-GT ratio).}
The fraction of path pixels that fall within the ground-truth sidewalk mask:
\begin{equation}
\text{Alignment} = \frac{L_{\text{in}}}{L_{\text{total}}} \times 100\%
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Temporal stability.}
Frame-to-frame IoU between consecutive cleaned masks. Frames below a threshold are flagged as ``unstable.'' The unstable rate captures temporal flicker.

\paragraph{Runtime.}
Per-module wall-clock time in milliseconds, measured on CPU to reflect embedded deployment conditions.

\section{Overnight Containment Validation}
\label{sec:overnight_eval}

An overnight automated evaluation systematically tested the turn containment guard under three configurations on VID\_017 with persistent right intent (300 frames):

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Strict guard} (ignore first 1 sample): rejected all turn paths as too strict, falling back entirely to DT corridor.
  \item \textbf{Projection recovery}: attempted to project samples back into the mask before refitting. Insufficient alone because failures originated from near-ego BEV mask edge noise, not far-field arc drift.
  \item \textbf{Near-ego exclusion} (ignore first 4 samples): recovered the original waypoint-turn window while achieving 0\% containment failure rate.
\end{enumerate}

The final \texttt{TURN\_PATH\_IGNORE\_FIRST\_SAMPLES\,=\,4} parameter accounts for the noisy BEV bottom-edge zone present when ego anchoring is disabled. With the scheduled intent window (right intent only on frames 20--30), the system achieved clean template-to-turn-to-template transitions with no containment failures, no path-outside pixels, and a minimum boundary clearance of \SI{4.775}{\px}.

\chapter{Discussion}
\label{ch:discussion}

\section{Interpretation of Key Findings}

The central finding of this thesis is that \emph{simple image-space geometry consistently outperforms the more complex BEV pipeline} in both accuracy and latency for monocular sidewalk navigation. This result is both an engineering improvement and a stronger scientific contribution than the initial hypothesis (that BEV skeleton planning would be the best approach).

The image-space midpoint planner achieves 14.3~px lateral center error at 2.2~ms---a $421\times$ speedup over BEV DT planning (65.0~px, 926.8~ms). This advantage persists even with oracle masks, confirming that the BEV planning domain itself introduces error rather than just inheriting segmentation noise.


\section{Why Image-Space Outperforms BEV}

The fundamental issue is geometric coverage. A monocular camera with typical field of view (55--70$^\circ$) maps only a narrow trapezoidal strip of the ground plane into the BEV grid. At typical scooter camera heights (0.8--1.2~m), most of the 600$\times$600 BEV grid is empty. The distance transform and skeleton algorithms then operate on a tiny sliver of occupied pixels---often less than 0.02\% of the BEV area---frequently failing to produce a connected path of sufficient length.

Image-space planning avoids this issue entirely. The segmentation mask fills a substantial portion of the image (typically 20--60\% of pixels), providing dense geometric information for boundary detection and midpoint extraction. The per-row processing naturally follows the camera's perspective geometry, and no lossy coordinate transformation is needed.

BEV projection also assumes flat ground and fixed camera pose. On real sidewalks, curbs, ramps, speed bumps, and camera vibration violate these assumptions, introducing geometric distortion that is absent in image-space processing.


\section{Segmentation as a Necessary but Not Sufficient Condition}

Better segmentation helps all planners: the improved model reduces temporal instability by 77\%, increases template success rate, and raises mask--path alignment across the board. However, BEV planning fails even with oracle masks (Table~\ref{tab:oracle_comparison}). Segmentation improvement has diminishing returns for BEV planning because the bottleneck is geometric coverage, not mask quality. In contrast, segmentation improvement directly and linearly benefits image-space planning.


\section{Template Planning and Turn Safety}

The template arc planner and waypoint-turn planner address two different failure modes of the baseline system. The template planner targets \emph{heading jitter}: by selecting from a fixed arc bank with family reuse hysteresis instead of per-frame graph search, heading oscillation drops by 40.6\% and path-source switching drops by 50\%. This improvement arises because the arc bank restricts the solution space to smooth, ego-anchored trajectories that are inherently less noisy than pixel-level Dijkstra paths.

The waypoint-turn planner targets \emph{junction navigation}: when GPS intent signals a turn, the corridor-support scan detects the opening and the circular arc provides a geometrically consistent maneuver path. The containment guard adds a critical safety layer by verifying the final committed path against the actual BEV mask---ensuring that planner confidence alone does not gate safety. The 0\% containment failure rate in validation suggests the guard is effective, though it is intentionally conservative: when a turn path clips the mask boundary, the system prefers safe hold behavior over an unsafe maneuver.

The 1800-frame accepted run (100\% template success, 0.954 IoU) demonstrates that the combined system---improved segmentation, template planner, and waypoint-turn planner---can sustain stable operation over extended sequences. However, the validation remains concentrated on a single campus route (VID\_017), and broader generalization is not yet established.


\section{Limitations}

Several limitations qualify the findings of this thesis:

\paragraph{Offline evaluation only.} All experiments are conducted offline on recorded video. The planned paths are evaluated against ground-truth masks but are never executed on the physical scooter. Path quality metrics (center error, inside-GT ratio) are necessary but not sufficient for navigation safety.

\paragraph{Limited hand-annotated sample.} The planner comparison uses 32 hand-annotated frames---a common benchmark size in robotics, but small by machine learning standards. The full-video replay across 22,679 frames partially compensates, and trends are consistent across sequences.

\paragraph{Fixed camera geometry.} All experiments use a single camera mount position and angle. The homography is calibrated for this specific configuration. Different camera heights, angles, or lens characteristics would require recalibration and may affect the relative performance of BEV-domain methods.

\paragraph{No explicit obstacle modeling.} YOLOv8n detection exists in the system but is not integrated into the planner cost function. Dynamic obstacles are handled only via implicit mask avoidance.

\paragraph{Single campus environment.} All data comes from the University of Oklahoma campus. Generalization to other cities, surfaces (brick, gravel, dirt), weather conditions (rain, snow), and lighting (nighttime) is unknown.

\paragraph{Conservative turn behavior.} The turn containment guard is intentionally conservative: if GPS intent remains latched after the maneuver window closes, the system prefers hold behavior rather than forcing a turn whose path leaves the mask. This design prioritizes safety over liveness but means that stale or stuck intent can cause unnecessary stops. The containment validation has been concentrated on VID\_017; behavior on videos 018--020 has not yet been verified.


\section{Threats to Validity}

\paragraph{Internal validity.} The same campus videos are used for both training and some evaluation sequences, though held-out splits and hand-annotated frames mitigate this concern. The temporal smoothing ablation uses the same EMA infrastructure being evaluated.

\paragraph{External validity.} Results may not generalize to non-campus environments, different scooter platforms, or significantly different camera configurations.

\paragraph{Construct validity.} Center error and inside-GT ratio are proxies for navigation quality. A path that scores well on these metrics might still be unsuitable for real-world navigation if it is temporally unstable or induces uncomfortable steering behavior.

\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{ch:conclusion}
% =====================================================================


\section{Summary of Contributions}

This thesis presented a modular vision-based sidewalk navigation pipeline that evolves through three design iterations, from a skeleton-graph baseline through distance-transform corridor planning to a lightweight image-space architecture. Through systematic comparison of five planning methods, we arrive at a result that is both an engineering improvement and a scientific finding: \emph{for monocular sidewalk navigation on embedded platforms, simple image-space geometry consistently outperforms the more complex BEV pipeline in accuracy, reliability, and latency.}


\section{Key Findings}

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item The OneFormer-trained SegFormer-B0 student achieves hand-annotated IoU of 0.946 at \SI{11.7}{ms}, improving over the baseline (0.758, \SI{18.9}{ms}).
  \item Image-space midpoint planning achieves 14.3~px lateral center error at \SI{2.2}{ms}---$421\times$ faster than BEV DT planning (65.0~px, \SI{926.8}{ms}).
  \item BEV-only planning fails on 99.3\% of frames in one profiled sequence due to insufficient monocular mask coverage.
  \item The recommended image-space architecture runs the full perception-to-path stack at over 59~FPS on CPU.
  \item Full-video evaluation across \numprint{22679} frames confirms that improved segmentation reduces temporal instability from 1.46\% to 0.33\%.
  \item The template arc planner reduces mean heading error by 40.6\% (from $1.190^\circ$ to $0.707^\circ$) and path-source switches by 50\% relative to the skeleton-graph baseline on a calibrated 220-frame evaluation clip.
  \item A GPS-conditioned waypoint-turn planner achieves 0\% turn containment failure rate on a 300-frame validation sequence while maintaining 12.9~FPS, and a 1800-frame accepted run achieves 100\% template success across all frames at a mean segmentation IoU of 0.954.
\end{enumerate}

The final recommended architecture---image-space midpoint primary, image-space DT fallback, BEV reserved for optional visualization and obstacle projection---is transparent, lightweight, and deployable on single-board computers. Each stage produces an interpretable intermediate representation that field teams can inspect and tune without retraining models.


\section{Future Work}

\paragraph{Closed-loop deployment.} The most critical next step is deploying the complete stack on the physical scooter with real-time obstacle detection, measuring actual tracking error with ground-truth trajectories, and studying failure modes at scale.

\paragraph{Temporal video segmentation.} Video-level consistency mechanisms (temporal attention, optical flow guidance) could further reduce frame-to-frame segmentation flicker beyond what EMA smoothing achieves.

\paragraph{Multi-camera or depth fusion.} Adding a depth sensor or stereo pair would enable metric obstacle distance estimation without the pinhole approximation, while maintaining the monocular pipeline as the primary perception path.

\paragraph{Global route planning.} The waypoint-turn planner introduced in this work provides GPS-conditioned junction behavior, but the GPS integration is coarse (left/right/straight intent from bearing comparison). A full global route planner with topological map, intersection detection, and multi-waypoint sequencing would enable autonomous multi-block navigation.

\paragraph{Cross-environment generalization.} Testing on diverse sidewalk environments (different cities, weather conditions, surface materials, nighttime) would strengthen the external validity of the findings.

\paragraph{Larger annotated benchmark.} Expanding the hand-annotated evaluation set from 32 to 100+ frames, with additional annotators for inter-rater agreement, would provide stronger statistical evidence for the planner comparison.
